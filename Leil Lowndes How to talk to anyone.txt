Leil Lowndes - How to talk to anyone



The idea or content of what you say is sometimes less important than the way you express it. The story you tell may not be a big deal, but how you speak—your tone, voice, expressions, and facial cues—can matter more. These things attract people. Without them, you may receive callous responses, like being ignored. This is especially true when people meet you for the first time; they tend to focus more on how you say something rather than what you say.

Avoid speaking in blunt or curt sentences.

Try to think of different ways to respond to the same question (like "How are you?").

Give the spotlight to the person you're interacting with—talk moderately about yourself and let the other person speak more.
